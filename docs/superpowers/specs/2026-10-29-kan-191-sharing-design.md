# KAN-191 approved workspace sharing policy

Policy approved by Calix Tan on 2026-10-29 in the ticket implementation conversation: “yes i like the policy area you've come up with”. This document records the approved proposal; enforcement is deferred until reviewed migration and cutover. KAN-191 owns the decision and resulting design scope, while KAN-178 owns deployed access verification.

Each customer workspace belongs durably to one customer. Ordinary customer access requires a verified identity, an active seat at that customer and explicit workspace membership. Only a workspace owner with access may invite active seat holders of that customer. Members cannot grant access. Cross-customer access requires an explicit recorded platform-admin exception scoped to a named principal and workspace; it does not waive active-seat, membership, or capability checks. An exception to access does not confer invitation authority. Platform administration retains its separate owner-checked admin context, not a blanket customer-app bypass.

Legacy workspaces must be inventoried and reviewed before restrictions take effect. Default/public, platform/internal, development demo and customer-owned demo workspaces need explicit classification. Missing ownership, password login, demo flags, creator's present seat, and email domain must not infer ownership or grant customer access. KAN-59 password test customers follow customer rules. No content deletion, automatic reassignment or silent lockout is authorized.

Approaches considered: retain any-member cross-customer sharing (simple but delegates customer disclosure broadly); owner-controlled external collaboration (more flexible but requires guest administration); chosen customer isolation with recorded admin exceptions (clear default boundary with deliberate support/collaboration exceptions).

Deliverables: an accepted ADR under specs/adr describing the policy, current versus intended behavior, access matrix and staged migration/enforcement scope; a small executable reference specification under specs/policies plus Vitest allowed/denied cases. The reference is design evidence only: no production imports, database migrations, runtime guards, UI or current access changes in this ticket. Tests must say they verify the intended policy model, not deployed enforcement. Production adapters and route/database/browser verification belong to subsequent enforcement work after migration readiness.

Rollout requirements: inventory; reviewed durable ownership/classification backfill; recorded exceptions and remediation; central request-time decisions across creation/listing/selection/pages/APIs/invites and trusted jobs; report-only comparison; explicit cutover approval; reversible per-workspace rollout with audit and recovery. Unresolved workspaces stay on existing behavior until their reviewed cutover; after cutover missing ownership denies ordinary access. Rollback pauses cutover and restores reviewed grants without reopening arbitrary sharing or dropping customer data.
