# Accuracy: review evidence through a residual decision

Open the existing Accuracy workspace and use Sources, Ledger, Coverage and Plan in order. AI output is a suggestion until a person confirms it with a rationale.

1. **Sources:** upload and parse the evidence, then extract needs and inventory. The saved source progress shows completed/failed units, full-source completeness, page attempts and the remaining cursor. An incomplete run can be resumed with **Retry remaining pages**, including after reloading the page. A changed source marks its checkpoint stale; start a new extraction. Omission review/pause still applies before downstream work.
2. **Ledger:** inspect each structured fact, its unknown reason and quoted evidence. Evidence links open the existing source preview at the cited block; resolved supporting documents open their source preview. Unresolved references remain labelled unresolved. Validate the actual facts with a rationale. The card separately shows validation freshness, computed coverage status and effective status, including a stale override that needs review.
3. **Coverage:** assess each page through the final page. Totals describe the complete pair snapshot, not just the current page. Pending means no current assessment; Limited is a real verdict. A model verdict alone does not establish human-validated coverage. Use **Pick a pair** to make a manual decision, including tactics beyond the first three. Proposed, cancelled and unknown-lifecycle tactics cannot address a gap, even with Full coverage. Source/factual changes invalidate stale assessments and cursors; follow the error's reload/restart instruction rather than assuming completion.
4. **Resolve Partial gap:** on a currently validated Partial card, load current split inputs and either suggest or prepare a manual split. Review both exact statements, direct evidence, committed closing tactics and residual dimensions. Enter a rationale and explicitly check the review confirmation before applying. The original is retired and operation history names the addressed and Open residual children. The residual inherits neither confirmed coverage nor human priority.
5. **Plan → Review S8 priority:** load current inputs, saved axes and their directions, evidence, planning context and missing limitations. To select another scope/axis pair, select it, reload its inputs, then save the Accuracy configuration with a rationale. A fresh suggestion and the persisted human working decision are displayed separately. High, Medium, Low and Defer are supported. Saving a working decision leaves it unvalidated; **Validate working priority** records the human band and rationale against the current facts/configuration. Changed facts or planning configuration retain the old human decision and show stale validation until reviewed again. The older metadata priority controls remain draft compatibility controls; they do not grant S8 validation.
6. **Ledger → Split operation history:** review the operation, enter a rollback rationale and explicitly confirm restoration. A clean split can restore the original and retire its children. A later human placement, validation or other protected child decision blocks rollback; the actionable message explains that the decisions are preserved. Resolve that situation through a new reviewed edit instead of deleting decisions to force an inverse.

All new inputs, review checkboxes, selectors and buttons support keyboard operation. With AI off, manual split preparation and S8 working/validation controls remain available. Provider failure leaves manual decisions available and explains retry/reload actions; a failed request does not confirm a split or priority decision.

The KAN83 browser proof uses deterministic responses from a local HTTP provider in the E2E harness, actual uploaded source blocks and a password-authenticated fixture owner. It checks integration and confirmation boundaries, not live-model extraction or scoring quality. Existing S6/S8 feature suites keep their established harness. Production code contains no fixture provider fallback.
